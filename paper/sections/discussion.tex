\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the result does and does not say.} Under matched meta supervision, adaptation budget and
batch membership, the order penalty did not add utility in this synthetic implementation. Its only
observable effect was to change the selected subspace in one of three world seeds, and there the change hurt.
This non-support is not evidence that the penalty has zero effect, and it is not evidence that it is
equivalent to the controls. It says nothing about order regularization in general or about image-based TTA.

\paragraph{The baseline had no adaptation gain.} The EM-toy baseline was worse than no adaptation on the
stationary terminal holdout in every seed, and no arm reached the gain MIE. Our hypothesis concerns
\emph{retaining} adaptation gain while reducing order sensitivity. An environment in which adaptation does
not help cannot evaluate that hypothesis adequately. This is a design limitation of the environment, not the
outcome of a formal power analysis, which we did not perform. Choosing an environment in which the baseline
adapts usefully is left as planned work (Appendix~\ref{app:planned}).

\paragraph{Discrete pool and greedy selection.} The candidate chooses among $2\times\res{dim}$ fixed
orthonormal pool directions with a greedy, early-stopping rule. With rank \res{ranks} selected everywhere,
the space of distinct choices was small, which makes coincidence with the utility-only control likely. A
continuous or larger pool could behave differently; we did not test one.

\paragraph{Learning-rate grid boundary.} The candidate's development-selected learning rate was the grid
maximum in \res{ncandbound} of $9$ condition--seed pairs. Selecting the upper boundary does not show that
the optimum was found, and all learning-rate-dependent comparisons inherit this caveat. We kept the grid
fixed rather than widening it after seeing results.

\paragraph{Few worlds and sampled orders.} There are \res{nseeds} world seeds and \res{norders} sampled
orders per seed. The worst observed order is not a bound on the risk over all orders. We report per-seed
paired differences instead of confidence intervals over pooled orders.

\paragraph{Supervised meta access.} The candidate and the utility-only control are fitted with labelled meta
data under simulated shifts, and development tuning reads development-holdout labels for every tuned arm.
None of the adapting arms reads test labels, but the subspace arms are not fully source-free, and the meta
shifts are only assumed to resemble the test shifts.

\paragraph{Model and optimizer scope.} The model is linear with $2\times\res{dim}$ adapted parameters,
normalization uses fixed source statistics, and the optimizer is plain SGD with a constant learning rate. The
analysis in Section~\ref{sec:analysis} is local, and we make no statement about Adam, long horizons or
batch-statistics dynamics.
